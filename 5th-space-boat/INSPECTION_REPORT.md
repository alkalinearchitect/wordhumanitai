## Verdict (honest)
**Renovatable, NOT a wreck — but not "free."** The steel shell is the expensive part and
it's sound. The real cost is the **internal timber (deck/frames) + systems (electrical,
roof, rails, certification)**. As a *static* community vessel (no need to run the engine
or be navigable), scope is reduced: no engine recommissioning, no hull painting below
waterline strictly required for static mooring.

## Biggest financial risk
Internal timber decay (deck/frames). A marine survey must probe this BEFORE commitment.
If frames are locally rotten, they are sister-ed/replaced — manageable. If widespread,
cost rises. The steel hull caps the downside.

## Red flags that block CIC use until fixed
1. Rope/low rail — fail public-liability safety.
2. Exposed wiring near water — shock/fire hazard.
3. Wet bilge + unknown timber — must be dried and surveyed.
4. Roof leak — must be sealed to stop internal rot.

## Next gates (in order)
1. **Marine survey** (hull + timber probe) — ~£300–£600. Decides go/no-go.
2. Confirm **mooring + planning** for static community use at the location.
3. Scope renovation + cost.
4. Fund via Awards for All (pilot) → Reaching Communities (scale).
