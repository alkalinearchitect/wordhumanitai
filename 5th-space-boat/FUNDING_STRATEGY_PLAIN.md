# How We Fund The 5th Space Boat — Plain English

> The boat is free to acquire. Making it safe and usable is the project. This is the
> simple path to pay for that with grants — written so anyone can understand it.

## The one-sentence version
We get the boat surveyed, then use a National Lottery grant to pay volunteers to renovate
it into a calm space for people under pressure. The boat is the asset; the grant pays the
work.

## Step 1 — Check it's worth it (the survey)
Before we commit, a marine surveyor checks the steel shell and the timber inside.
Cost: about £300–£600 (ESTIMATE — verify with local surveyors).
Why: the steel hull looks sound (we saw it lifted by a crane, which only a solid hull
survives). The risk is the internal wood. The survey decides yes/no.
*This is the only money we risk up front.*

## Step 2 — The easy win: Awards for All (National Lottery)
- **What:** £300 to £20,000. No deadline — apply any time.
- **Who:** CICs like ours. We are eligible (No. 16891121).
- **Decision:** about 16 weeks.
- **How we frame it (plain):** "We're turning a donated boat into a community space where
  people learn trades, volunteer, and find calm. The grant pays the materials and the
  supervised work."
- **Why it works:** funders like visible, local, community-led projects. A boat renovation
  is all three. Volunteers = low labour cost = your money goes far.

## Step 3 — The bigger one: Reaching Communities (National Lottery)
- **What:** £20,001 to £20 million. Up to 5 years.
- **When:** after Awards for All proves the model works.
- **Use:** fit it out properly — safe rails, certified electrics, heating, accessible ramp,
  a staffed programme.

## Step 4 — Local money
Many councils and combined authorities have small community grants for "meanwhile" assets
and wellbeing spaces. We apply to the one where the boat will sit.

## What the boat is NOT (honesty rule)
We do NOT claim it's running, seaworthy, or open. On the website and in applications it is
clearly: **"concept / under renovation / not yet in service."** This protects us and the
funder. The grant funds the journey from shell to space.

## Biggest risk & how we handle it
Internal timber decay. Handled by: survey first, localised repair (sistering/replacing
ribs), keep the boat static (no engine or navigation needed). The steel hull limits the
downside — a rotten timber hull would be a dealbreaker; a rotten timber deck is fixable.
